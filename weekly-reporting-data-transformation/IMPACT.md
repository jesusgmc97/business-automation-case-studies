# Operational Impact Analysis

The workflow operates on a weekly reporting cycle, equivalent to approximately **52 executions per year**.

Because the original process includes several manual stages, the annual operational impact depends primarily on the amount of time required for each reporting cycle before automation.

## Annual scaling model

| Manual effort per weekly cycle | Annual manual effort | Equivalent working days* |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 30 minutes | 26 hours | 3.7 days |
| 1 hour | 52 hours | 7.4 days |
| 1.5 hours | 78 hours | 11.1 days |
| 2 hours | 104 hours | 14.9 days |
| 3 hours | 156 hours | 22.3 days |

\*Using a 7-hour working day for illustration.

This table shows why a weekly process can generate substantial annual savings even when each individual execution appears relatively small.

## Automated stages

The workflow reduces or removes manual work associated with:

- Source-file review
- CSV restructuring
- Data cleaning
- Data type conversion
- Excel table preparation
- Repetitive calculations and transformations
- Reporting-file preparation
- Email distribution
- Internal document publication

## Value beyond time savings

The operational value is not limited to hours saved.

Automation also improves:

- Consistency between reporting cycles
- Repeatability of data transformations
- Reduction of manual formatting errors
- Standardization of outputs
- Traceability of execution
- Reliability of report distribution
- Scalability if reporting volume increases

## Measurement approach

A precise annual saving can be calculated by comparing:

```text
Annual manual effort
=
Average manual processing time per cycle
x
52 weekly cycles
```

and:

```text
Annual net time saved
=
Annual manual effort
-
Annual supervision / exception-handling time
```

Only validated operational measurements should be used when presenting a specific percentage reduction or financial saving publicly.
